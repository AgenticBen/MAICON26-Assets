# **MAICON Opening session**

Wed, 14 Oct 26

#### **Conference Context**

* MAICON 2026, Cleveland: \~2,000 attendees, 25+ countries, 48 states  
* Day three of events (AI Council Monday, CMO Summit Tuesday, workshops \+ party Tuesday evening)  
* Speaker: Paul Roetzer, CEO of Smarter X and the Marketing AI Institute

#### **The Core Problem: The Broken Talent Ladder**

* Traditional model: junior staff did tactical work, learned by doing, graduated to strategy  
* AI now does everything an entry-level marketer did in 2000, for \$20/month, faster and better  
* The ladder from novice to expert is breaking  
  * Junior lawyers, consultants, marketers built expertise through repetitive tactical work  
  * That work is now absorbed by AI or done directly by senior staff with domain expertise  
* Central question: if AI does the work people once learned from, how do organizations develop future managers, experts, and executives?

#### **AI Capability Trajectory**

* OpenAI’s five levels: chatbots (2022) → reasoning (2024) → agents (now) → innovation → organizational AI  
* Current moment: agent takeoff, becoming more reliable and autonomous  
* Jensen Huang (NVIDIA): GPT-6.1 abstract is AGI; OpenAI signals “we are in the AGI era”  
* High-confidence predictions:  
  * Labs will continue accelerating toward AGI  
  * Within 12 months: AI at A-player level on any task taking a senior marketer 5+ hours  
  * By MAICON 2027: majority of marketing work automatable by agents  
* Adoption is jagged: some teams racing ahead, others (finance, legal, HR) barely started

#### **How Work Is Compressing**

* Senior staff with domain expertise can now do the full production work of a junior team  
  * Roetzer built the AI Score book and program largely solo, using Claude, on planes and in his basement  
* Cross-functional compression: marketers doing sales work, ops people doing design, etc.  
  * Creates internal friction as AI-forward employees outpace slower colleagues  
* Revenue-per-employee pressure rising: CFOs will ask why target isn’t $1M–$10M/employee  
  * AI-native companies already hitting \$10–20M revenue per employee  
* Upskilling at scale creates its own problem: review/approval capacity doesn’t scale with output

#### **The Architect, Orchestrator, and Apprentice Framework**

* Three evolving roles (not a complete staffing model, more an evolution of strategist/manager/IC):  
  * **Architect**: designs systems, defines strategy and human-machine operating models  
    * Stripe’s “deployed AI accelerator” role cited as the best real-world example  
    * Embedded in cohorts of 25–45 marketers, measures success by workflows permanently transformed  
  * **Orchestrator**: runs the systems, directs agents and human collaborators, evaluates outputs  
    * Skills: model/tool selection, data integration, token budget management, human-in-the-loop governance  
  * **Apprentice**: learns within the system through observation, direction, and experimentation  
    * Develops judgment, taste, ethics, institutional knowledge, emotional intelligence  
    * Needs mentorship, shadowing, frameworks for evaluating AI output quality  
    * Needs access to chain-of-thought: seeing how decisions are made, not just the outputs  
* Key Shopify CEO (Toby Lütke) quote: “The risk is not that AI does the work. The risk is that AI does the work and we never learn from it. If every interaction with an agent happens in a private window, the only person who learns anything is the person at the keyboard.”
