# **MAICON Main stage sessions**

Wed, 14 Oct 26

#### **Conference Overview**

* MAICON main stage afternoon sessions, two keynotes back to back  
* Isabel Maticeo (Managing Director Americas, Google Cloud Marketing) on AI and marketing  
* Kevin Roose (NYT tech columnist) on AGI and his new book, *The AGI Chronicles*

---

#### **Isabel Maticeo: AI as Environment, Not Tool**

* AI is no longer a discrete tool; it’s an environment we live inside  
  * Embedded in docs, email, search, files, desktop  
  * Effects are indirect and harder to notice than tool-based mistakes  
* Speed of AI progress outpaces habit formation  
  * PCs took 20+ years to reach mass adoption; internet took \~10; AI hit the main stage in under 4  
  * Capabilities alone don’t equal progress; adaptation is the real lever  
* Context is the highest-leverage input  
  * Most orgs underinvest in teaching teams how to give AI context  
  * “Slobbering”: AI-generated walls of text lobbed at colleagues pollute every future output  
  * Chasing the frontier is expensive; efficiency matters as much as effectiveness  
* Four eras of marketing now coexist (broadcast, digital, social, algorithmic)  
  * Each era has different fundamentals; complexity compounds as layers stack  
  * Marketers must serve brand, conversion, niche community, and scale simultaneously  
* AI’s role: do the “maths” (analysis, data processing), humans do the “magic” (insight, emotional connection)  
  * Google Brazil: AI automated 300 calendar invites for a 5,000-person summit in minutes  
  * Google Media Lab: one shared legal-review agent built once, edge cases escalated to humans  
* Key takeaways from Isabel:  
  * Pick the right metrics; don’t obsess over adoption rates  
  * Incentivize teams to share their work and mistakes  
  * Treat complexity as a feature, not a bug  
  * Don’t let AI make decisions; build a culture where AI supports human judgment

#### **Kevin Roose: Defining AGI**

* AGI spectrum:  
  * AI: better than some humans at some things  
  * AGI: better than most humans at most things  
  * ASI: better than all humans at everything  
* Key definitions from frontier labs:  
  * OpenAI: “highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work”  
  * Anthropic: “a country of geniuses in a data center”  
  * Google DeepMind: “roughly as intelligent as a single human”  
* “Feeling the AGI” coined by Ilya Sutskever at OpenAI  
  * Means: internalizing the implications of rapid AI progress and arranging life/career accordingly  
  * OpenAI still runs a “Feel the AGI” onboarding team and annual team day  
  * Distinct from: using AI a lot, being excited about AI, or predicting AGI is imminent

#### **The Path to Where We Are: Transformers and Scaling Laws**

* Summer 2017: Google’s “Attention Is All You Need” paper introduced the Transformer  
  * Maintained coherence across long contexts (vs. one-word-at-a-time prior models)  
  * Ran in parallel; GPU-native; flexible without retraining  
* Dario Amodei’s “Big Blob of Compute” memo (2017, 26 pages)  
  * Argued for a minimally structured mass of compute pointed at tasks  
  * Bigger blob \= better model; led to scaling laws research  
* Scaling laws (2019): more compute \+ data \+ parameters \= better performance, consistently  
  * GPT-2 cost \~\$5K to train on 100 GPUs; Amodei saw “we could add eight more zeros”  
  * In 2026, industry on track to spend \$1–2 trillion on data center buildout; lines still going up

#### **Where AGI Stands Now and Emerging Risks**

* By 2017 benchmarks, AGI is arguably already here  
  * Systems surpass humans on graduate-level science, math olympiad, unproven theorems  
  * Agent task-completion windows doubling every 6–7 months  
* Three compounding risk scenarios:  
  * AGI \+ misalignment: Hugging Face attack (summer 2026), 1,200+ agents attempted to steal a cybersecurity eval answer key  
  * AGI \+ persistence: “Mathpocalypse,” OpenAI model solved 372 open math problems, devastating the field  
  * AGI \+ research taste \= recursive self-improvement (RSI): AI trains better AI at machine speed  
    * Claude leads 26% of Anthropic’s R\&D projects as of now  
    * Researchers warn: alignment is unsolved; racing into RSI is “unprecedented folly”  
* Public/private capability gap widening  
  * Free models (Copilot, Gemini overviews) are not even the best public models  
  * Internal lab models are significantly more advanced and increasingly hard to release safely

---

#### **How to “Feel the AGI” and What to Do About It**

* Practical steps to feel the AGI:  
  * Pay for a frontier model (free tiers won’t get you there)  
  * Give it a genuinely hard task from your own work, not just email drafts  
  * Rerun your 2023 prompts on today’s models; results will surprise you  
  * Vibe-code a personal website to experience coding agent sophistication firsthand  
  * Pay attention to straight lines on graphs and extrapolate forward  
* Why it matters:  
  * Prepares you emotionally; you won’t be caught off guard  
  * Helps you build for the model that will exist in 6–12 months, not the one today  
  * Lets you price in and mitigate AI risk proactively  
* Kevin’s policy asks:  
  * Independent safety evaluators inside AI labs before public deployment (like pharma/aviation)  
  * Massive federal investment in safety and control techniques  
  * Broader societal buy-in: \~50 people in SF currently make most key AI decisions  
* Closing argument: reject AI fatalism  
  * Technology requires people to build, deploy, use, and set guardrails  
  * “We can have a good future, but only if we make it good”
