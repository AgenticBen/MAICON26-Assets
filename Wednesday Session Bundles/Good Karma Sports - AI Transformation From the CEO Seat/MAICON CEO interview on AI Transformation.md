# **MAICON CEO interview on AI Transformation**

Wed, 14 Oct 26

#### **Good Karma Sports: AI Transformation Story**

* Craig Carmen, founder/CEO of Good Karma Sports (500 employees, 12 offices, ESPN’s official digital partner)  
* Ty Bashek, Senior Director of Innovation and Sales Development  
* Company has been around 29 years; previously transformed from radio to digital (3% to \$50M digital revenue in 6 years)  
* AI transformation began \~1 year ago; now 65%+ of staff using AI daily

#### **How the Transformation Happened**

* Craig’s catalyst: first paid AI model session convinced him the company wouldn’t exist in 3 years without going all in  
  * Read “The Coming Wave” by Mustafa Suleyman twice; sent it to all leadership  
* Announced company-wide on a Friday all-hands call: embrace AI or likely lose your job within a year  
  * Framed it as empowerment, not threat: “you have permission to play, permission to fail”  
  * Rolled out 500 licenses; crowdsourced best use cases rather than dictating from the top  
* Rolled out 40 licenses at a time; created an AI council; ran 5 weekly AI training calls  
  * Quarterly “back to basics” sessions covering connectors, plugins, memory, SharePoint  
* Craig personally spends 50%+ of device time on AI  
  * Example: financial model for a major league opportunity done in 15 minutes vs. 24 hours expected  
  * Freed up time for 19 face-to-face meetings in one LA office visit

#### **Innovation Team, Results, and What’s Next**

* Two-person innovation team (Max and Gina) promoted from within after excelling in their own AI use  
  * Had been innovating in 20% of their time; Craig’s reaction: “what would they do with 100%?”  
  * Departments now have a waiting list to work with the innovation team  
* Three impact areas:  
  * Built custom software (digital billing and planning systems) after failing to find a suitable vendor  
  * Modernized workflows: getting the company “out of Excel”  
  * Reimagined campaign building: 2-3 hours per campaign reduced to 10 minutes  
* Benchmarking standard before scaling: time savings alone aren’t enough  
  * Target: faster AND 10%+ better quality output before rolling out as company standard  
  * Expansion of innovation specialists planned for 2027, tied to rewritten job descriptions  
* Hardest lessons:  
  * Craig painted with too broad a brush early on; some “superstar” roles genuinely transcend AI  
  * Middle managers not bought in create bottlenecks that block discovery from reaching leadership  
  * Keeping pace with AI’s rapid evolution is the ongoing operational challenge  
* Hiring philosophy now: entry-level hires treated as AI-native from day one  
  * Craig personally interviews all full-time hires to screen for AI openness  
  * Created 3 new roles that didn’t exist before the transformation  
* Closing advice: “have patience urgently” (Ty); AI raised the ceiling of the hungriest people, not just the floor (Craig)
