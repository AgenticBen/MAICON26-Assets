# **MAICON AI scaling: impacts and alternatives**

Thu, 15 Oct 26

#### **The Empire Analogy: AI’s Structural Critique**

* Speaker (Karen Howe, author) frames dominant AI companies as modern empires  
  * Extract extraordinary labor while workers see little return  
  * Monopolize AI research, controlling what gets published  
  * Justify expansion with moral/existential imperatives: “good empire vs. evil empire”  
* Scaling ideology drives the industry  
  * GPT models scaled 10,000x between 2019 and 2023 (data \+ compute)  
  * Utopians (accelerationists) and doomers both share the same scaling faith  
  * Sarah Hooker (Cohere): “Scaling is a cheap formula for getting more performance, intellectually cheap, highly imprecise”

#### **Harms of Aggressive Scaling**

* Training data harms  
  * GPT-2: curated articles; GPT-4: pirated books, indiscriminate web scraping, YouTube transcription (against ToS)  
  * Erodes IP, perpetuates engagement-centric social media dynamics, pollutes datasets  
  * Data annotation now the 4th fastest-growing US job (LinkedIn); gig conditions, no paid breaks, always-on  
* Psychological harm from engagement-optimized models  
  * Three teenagers/young adults died by suicide after toxic relationships with Character AI or ChatGPT  
  * Megan Garcia’s 14-year-old son sexually groomed by Character AI before his death  
  * Extended sessions bypass safety filters; companies knowingly keep users on longer than safe  
* Environmental cost of compute scaling  
  * Data centers used 448 TWh in 2025; ranked 11th globally if a country  
  * 189 million tons of CO2 equivalent, 4.5 trillion liters of fresh water consumed  
  * AI share projected to jump from \~20% to \~40% of that footprint by 2030  
  * 2/3 of data centers sited in water-stressed areas  
  * Amazon sought 48% water increase in drought-prone northern Spain, strategized to hide true usage  
  * Elon Musk’s xAI deployed 35 unlicensed methane turbines overnight in Tennessee with no community notice

#### **Alternatives: Bicycles vs. Rockets**

* Large-scale generative models are “rockets”: high cost, narrow fit, not needed for most use cases  
* “Bicycles”: small, purpose-built, domain-specific AI systems  
  * DeepMind AlphaFold: protein folding, Nobel Prize 2024, trained on curated data, no supercomputer needed  
  * Climate Change AI nonprofit catalogs purpose-built tools for emissions tracking, grid efficiency, weather forecasting  
* Open-weight models as a transition step  
  * Same capabilities, but usage doesn’t feed data back to train next-generation models  
  * Open-source (vs. open-weight) would allow scientists to inspect training data and model internals  
* Taheku Media (New Zealand) as the vision  
  * Maori-language radio station built a community-consented AI transcription tool  
  * Community education campaign first, then voluntary data donation drive; collected enough data in days  
  * Models now represent the frontier for Te Reo Maori; Taheku became a government service provider  
* Scaling is not driving innovation; it’s crowding it out  
  * Nearly half of all Q1 2024 VC went to just OpenAI and Anthropic, pulling capital from climate tech

#### **Signs of Change and Who to Follow**

* Grassroots wins  
  * Tucson city council voted 7-0 to block Amazon’s Project Blue data center  
  * Character AI declared bankruptcy, partly driven by Megan Garcia’s lawsuit  
  * OpenAI shut down Sora video generation (compute constraints tied to data center protests); attempts to revive it as a separate company  
* Positive trends noted  
  * Open-weight model adoption growing among companies and consumers  
  * More industries exploring domain-specific models rather than defaulting to ChatGPT/Claude  
* Personal stance on using generative AI (Karen’s three reasons for abstaining)  
  * Moral: doesn’t want to fund companies she’s investigated  
  * Pragmatic: her core skills (empathy-driven sourcing, compelling writing) aren’t aided by AI  
  * Operational security: investigative work on OpenAI would be surveilled via usage data  
* Resource: Culture Centers AI Accountability Network  
  * Thousands of journalists across 140+ countries reporting on AI’s real-world community impacts  
  * Weekly newsletter aggregating ground-level stories outside the Silicon Valley narrative
