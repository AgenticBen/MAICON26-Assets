# **MAICON Final session**

Thu, 15 Oct 26

#### **How Current Roles Are Evolving**

* Marketers expanding from individual skills to building and managing entire systems  
* “Architect” emerging as an evolved form of strategist  
  * Sees opportunity, understands AI capabilities, looks across workflows and team goals  
  * Not improving workflows but reinventing them entirely  
  * Distinct from the “builder” who spins up prototypes from the architect’s direction  
* Institutional knowledge becoming a fast track into AI-forward roles  
  * Example: 15-year project manager moved seamlessly into AIOps  
* Reseeding over redundancy: leading orgs reskilling people into new roles rather than cutting them  
  * Not always a fit (e.g., a career graphic designer pivoting to agent workflow design)  
  * But signals a clear values commitment to existing teams

#### **New Roles and the AI Transformation Function**

* “Orchestrator” emerging as a critical informal role: transforming the org, building systems, managing agents  
* Title matters less than the job description evolving  
  * Roles likely stay the same in name; responsibilities shift to include creating and governing agents  
  * “Forward Deployed Engineer” (FDE) gaining traction, especially in enterprise via Palantir  
* AI transformation leadership likely hybrid: centralized strategy plus ground-up empowerment  
  * Centralized for highest-value workflows (requires strategist mindset)  
  * Decentralized to surface hidden talent, like the person who did it for marketing and got asked to roll it out globally

#### **Skills That Stay Uniquely Human**

* Knowing what to ask AI, evaluating outputs, iterating prompts effectively  
  * Compounding value: the better the models get, the more this skill matters  
* Curiosity, imagination, and resilience  
  * “Unlearning” flagged as a distinct skill: willingness to move on from what you were good at 6 months ago  
* Critical thinking and the ability to defend your work  
  * AI can generate a strategy; being able to answer 10 hard “why” questions about it is the real test  
* Risk of skipping the craft: giving entry-level employees AI shortcuts before they know what “good” looks like  
  * CEOs on panels craving outputs that feel genuinely human, not copy-pasted AI

#### **Agents, Middle Management, and Entry-Level Work**

* Agent adoption will be a long curve, not a sudden shift  
  * Most large enterprises still teaching employees to use AI assistants  
  * Near-term agents: deterministic, rules-based (e.g., weekly analyst reports, automated data queries)  
  * Autonomous agent swarms running full go-to-market: possible at the frontier, not enterprise reality in 12 months  
* Middle management under pressure if lacking domain expertise and AI fluency  
  * Senior leaders with deep expertise \+ AI skills can absorb work previously done by layers below them  
  * Those in the 3-7 year range without established expertise face the hardest value proposition to defend  
* Entry-level: AI fluency increasingly outweighs seniority for new opportunities  
  * But AI is also reducing the volume of tasks available to learn from, fewer reps  
  * Apprenticeship model raised as a potential response  
* Closing advice: whatever your field, be the best at using AI within it  
  * Attendees framed as the “1%”; the broader challenge is the population that isn’t in the room
