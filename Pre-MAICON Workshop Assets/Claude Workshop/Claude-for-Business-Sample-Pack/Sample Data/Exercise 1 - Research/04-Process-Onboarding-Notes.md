# Process and documentation: sample material

Synthetic material for the Claude for Business workshop. Northwind Supply is invented.
These are deliberately messy, because that is what real process notes look like.

---

## Raw notes: "how we onboard a new distributor account"

Found in a shared drive. Author unknown. Last modified eleven months ago.

Sales sends over the signed agreement. Usually to me but sometimes to Dana, depends who
closed it. I check the credit application is attached, if it isn't I go back to sales and
that's usually where the first week goes.

Credit check goes to finance. Marcus runs it. Takes 2 to 5 business days, longer at month end
because he's closing books. If the account is under 50k we can skip the full check and use the
short form, but nobody remembers that so mostly we run the full one anyway.

Once credit clears I set them up in the ERP. Account number, payment terms, shipping defaults,
tax exemption cert if they have one. The tax cert is the thing that gets missed most often.
If it's missing we have to go back to the customer and it's awkward because by then they think
they're set up.

Then pricing. Tiered off annual committed volume. Dana handles this if it's a national account,
I handle regional. There's a spreadsheet. The spreadsheet has three tabs and only one is current.

Welcome email goes out from whoever set them up. There's no template, everyone writes their own.
Mine has the portal login, the rep's contact, and the lead time sheet attached. I don't know
what Dana's has.

First order usually comes in within two weeks. If it doesn't, someone should follow up, but
there's no trigger for that so it depends whether anyone notices.

We've had two accounts go six months without ordering and nobody caught it.

## What you can see without being told

There is no owner. There are two parallel paths depending on who closed the deal. There is a
shortcut nobody uses. There is a known failure point (the tax certificate) with no check in
front of it. There is a step that depends entirely on somebody noticing. And there is a
spreadsheet with three tabs where only one is current, which is its own small horror.

That is roughly the state of most undocumented processes. It is a good candidate for TRIPS.
