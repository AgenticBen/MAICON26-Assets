# Distributor account onboarding, as documented

## The short version

There are seven steps, and only one has a named owner: the credit check, run by Marcus. That assumes he's still in the role, because the notes are eleven months old. The author did most of the work and has left. So three steps now have no owner at all. Three more are covered only some of the time, depending on who closed the deal or what kind of account it is. **Three steps with no owner** is the finding. The rest of this document is what it takes to fix it.

## The process

"The author" below means whoever wrote the original notes. The notes don't give their name or role.

| # | Step | Owner in the notes | Owner now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales sends the signed agreement over | Sales sends it, "usually" to the author and "sometimes" to Dana, depending on who closed the deal | **Half.** Most handoffs went to the author, who has left |
| 2 | Check the credit application is attached. If it isn't, send it back to sales | The author. Possibly Dana for deals sent to her. The notes don't say | **None** |
| 3 | Credit check. Takes 2 to 5 business days, longer at month end. Accounts under 50k can use the short form | Marcus, Finance | Marcus, if he's still in the role |
| 4 | Set the account up in the ERP: account number, payment terms, shipping defaults, tax exemption certificate if they have one | The author. Possibly Dana for her deals. The notes don't say | **None** |
| 5 | Set pricing, tiered off annual committed volume, using the pricing spreadsheet | Dana for national accounts, the author for regional | **Half.** Nobody for regional accounts |
| 6 | Send a welcome email. No template. The author's had the portal login, the rep's contact and the lead time sheet. What Dana's contains is unknown | Whoever did the setup | **Half.** Dana sends her own. Nobody sends the author's |
| 7 | Follow up if the first order doesn't come in. The notes say first orders "usually" come within two weeks. No trigger is defined | Nobody. "Depends whether anyone notices" | **None** |

## Where it fails, ranked by what it costs

1. **Nobody follows up when an account doesn't order** (step 7). Two accounts went six months without ordering and nobody chased them. That's six months of orders from two accounts, and it's the only failure the notes count occurrences of.
2. **The tax exemption certificate gets missed** (step 4). The notes call it "the thing that gets missed most often." Fixing it means going back to a customer who thinks they're already set up. That's rework, and a bad first impression at the point we most want a good one.
3. **Pricing comes from a spreadsheet where only one of three tabs is current** (step 5). The notes don't say which tab. The risk is quoting a new account from the wrong tier. That's my inference. The notes don't say it has happened.
4. **The credit application is missing** (step 2). When it is, "that's usually where the first week goes." The notes don't say how often.
5. **The short-form credit check is mostly not used** (step 3). Accounts under 50k could use the short form, but "nobody remembers that," so most wait 2 to 5 business days for the full check, longer at month end.
6. **Two different splits** (steps 1, 5 and 6). Handoff and welcome email split by who closed the deal. Pricing splits by national or regional account. The notes don't say whether those line up.

## Three questions to answer before this is final

1. **Who owns this end to end, and who receives the signed agreement now that the author has left?** Every other fix depends on this one.
2. **What does "under 50k" measure?** The notes don't say whether it's the credit limit, annual committed volume or something else. Is the short form still allowed?
3. **Which tab of the pricing spreadsheet is current?** Once someone answers, mark the other two out of date or remove them.

## Also ambiguous, not resolved here

- What should trigger the step 7 follow-up.
- What Dana's welcome email contains.
- Whether Dana also does steps 2 and 4 for the deals she closes.
- Whether Marcus and Dana are still in these roles.
